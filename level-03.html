3.1

Twelve parts, twelve numbers

Take twelve parts off one machine — same tool, same operator, same gauge, one after another. what one machine didspan of twelve47 µmgauge resolution1 µmEvery reading is different, and nothing is broken. The twelve of them cover forty-seven microns, and two differ by a single micron, which is exactly where this gauge stops. Below that it has nothing to say.

Spread is not a defect. spoken · 0:21“Spread is not a defect. It is what every real process does, and the job is to describe it.”It is what every real process does, and the job of this chapter is to describe it with three numbers instead of twelve.

3.2

The mean is a balance point

Twelve numbers are not an answer; you need one number for the centre. the testA candidate centre is right when the deviations either side of it cancel exactly.So guess one and check it. Draw every part's distance to the guess, then add the distances on each side and see whether they cancel.

They do not, and the beam tips. spoken · 0:58“Not a formula you were handed — the one place where the deviations cancel.”So walk the guess along until they do. One position makes the two sides equal and opposite and the beam comes level. That position is the mean — not a formula you were handed, but the one place where the deviations cancel.

3.3

Spread has to be squared first

Now the spread. the spreadsigma, twelve parts12.7 µmAveraging those deviations is useless: we have just proved they cancel, by construction. Square each one instead. Negatives turn positive, the cancelling stops, and every square is an area you can put on a shelf.

Twelve squares averaged, then rooted, which puts the answer back into millimetres. That is σ\sigmaσ, and it is the side of the average square. For these twelve parts, 12.7 microns.

3.4

A shape nobody chose

Twelve parts say nothing about shape. keep goingparts measured20 000binsunchangedKeep measuring, and keep the same bins: a hundred parts, a thousand, twenty thousand off the same machine with the same gauge. A shape appears that nobody chose or asked for.

Many small independent effects, added together, land on this curve. The bell is a consequence of the process, not an assumption about it.

3.5

You never measure everything

The true centre and spread belong to the process itself. sample, not processTwelve parts estimate the centre and spread. They are not the centre and spread.Your twelve parts only estimate them, and the estimate is not the thing: four more handfuls of twelve, off the same untouched machine, land somewhere else every time.

It is also why a sample's spread divides by n − 1. why not ndivisorn − 1Twelve parts spread around their own mean sit a little tighter than they do around the truth, so the smaller divisor corrects for it. Every estimate carries uncertainty, and Level 4 puts a number on exactly how much.
