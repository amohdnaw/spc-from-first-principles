What I actually do

Most quality problems don't announce themselves. They drift. A tool wears, a supplier changes a batch, a setting creeps — and by the time final inspection catches it, you've made thousands of parts you can't ship.

My job is making sure it never gets that far: SPC systems end-to-end — rational subgroups, the right chart for the data, Gage R&R before anyone blames the process, capability studies (Cp/Cpk/Ppk), and reaction plans operators actually follow.

And because a chart nobody reads is worthless, I put equal work into the communication layer — visuals where the signal is impossible to miss, written for people who've never heard of a Western Electric rule. Try the labs below: every claim on this page, you can poke at yourself.

a.

Cutting diameter variation on a CNC line by half

A critical bore was failing Cpk audits (1.1) with no assignable cause. Layering an EWMA chart over the X̄-R exposed slow tool wear invisible to the Shewhart chart. Fixing coolant temperature cycling + tying tool changes to the EWMA signal brought the process back to center. Proving the measurement system before blaming the process

Two engineers had already tried — and failed — to fix a "chaotic" filling line. A crossed Gage R&R showed the measurement system itself consumed 61% of tolerance.
