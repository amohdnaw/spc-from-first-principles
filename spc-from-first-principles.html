SPC from First Principles

Why SPC works — derived, not asserted.

Most training teaches control charts as rules to memorise. They are not rules. Every formula on a shop floor is a consequence of one statistical idea, and each level below builds on the last — ending somewhere you can defend every number in front of an audit.

Nothing here is read off a table. The control-chart constants are derived by Monte Carlo simulation and then checked against AIAG Table B in a test suite, so the library is its own proof. Every figure and every frame of video on these pages was computed from that same code.

One die is chaos, the average of thirty is a bell exactly σ/√30 wide. σ/√n → Level II Control limits are a hypothesis test ±3σ isn't taste. If the process is stable, a point lands outside 0.27% of the time — so a violation is a bet at 370:1 that something changed. 370:1 → Level III Capability is comparing two distributions Spec limits are the customer's voice, the spread is the process's. Cpk prices the mismatch as an exact tail integral. Cpk → ppm → Level IV Detection theory: memory beats points Slow drift never looks alarming in any single point.
