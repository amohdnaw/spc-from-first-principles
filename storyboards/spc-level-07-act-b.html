SPC Level 7 · Act B · shot storyboard · locked before Manim

Which rule set earns its false alarms is a property of the line, not of the rules.

That sentence is the act's only visual claim. Five shots reveal one masked case, take a commitment before any total exists, price all three rule sets from that one case, move exactly one premise until the winner changes, and end on the action a shift supervisor takes. Every value on screen is read from a generated case; nothing is typed twice.

The case

One masked line, four facts

Approved copy for the act's surface story. It is stored once as a blueprint string in level07_mastery and reaches the act as challenge_case(CASE_SEEDS[0])["surface_story"]. It names no company, product, part, person, or equipment identifier, and it is written so that the two cost shapes are visible in the story before they are visible on screen.

Surface story

A masked deposition line plots one film-thickness subgroup every sampling interval. When the mean really drifts, everything produced between the drift and the alarm is reworked at the next step, so the damage is charged per minute the drift goes unseen. When the chart alarms and nothing has changed, the line stops for one check, so that damage is charged once per event.

The four case fields, in the exact labels the challenge and its feedback also use

expected shift size · false-alarm cost · missed-shift cost · sampling cadence

The three options, in the exact labels the challenge also uses

Rule 1 only · Rules 1 + 2 · All four Western Electric rules

Shot table

Five shots on one line strip

The strip built in B1 is the source of every block that later becomes a cost bar. Money is never introduced as a number; it is carried out of the strip that produced it.

Shot B1

Four facts, built before they are named

Beat 1Creates the model
Persistent object
The line strip: a horizontal run of subgroup slots with the process curve standing above it. The strip stays on screen for the whole act, and a four-row case card builds beside it, one row per field.
Learner prediction
None. A guess is worthless before the four facts exist, and B2 takes the commitment as soon as they do.
Transformation
Each field is built as a physical thing first and labelled second. The curve above the strip slides sideways and stays offset, which is the expected shift size. The slots space out to one measured interval and a hand sweeps exactly one slot, which is the sampling cadence. One slot turns amber and a single cost block rises out of it, which is the false-alarm cost. The slots after the offset fill red one at a time, each one adding another block to a growing stack, which is the missed-shift cost. Only when all four exist do the four labels settle onto the case card.
Surprise
The two costs are different shapes. The false-alarm block is one block per event and stops growing. The missed-shift stack keeps growing while nothing is done, because it is charged per minute. Nobody has to be told that waiting is the expensive one.
Counterexample
None. This shot only supplies the facts the rest of the act spends.
Formula reveal
None. No expected-cost expression is permitted until the bars in B3 exist to attach its terms to.
Readout source
challenge_case(CASE_SEEDS[0]) supplies surface_story, expected_shift_size, false_alarm_cost, missed_shift_cost, and sampling_cadence. The curve offset is drawn at expected_shift_size, which the blueprint pins to SHIFT from spclab.evidence, so Act A's one-sigma world and this case are the same world.
Camera move
One slow lateral track along the strip while the red slots fill, so the growing stack is felt as elapsed time rather than seen as a bar. The camera then settles wide with the strip and all four case rows in one frame.
Narration line
"Four facts about one line. Everything after this is arithmetic on those four."
Exit state
Strip, offset curve, one amber block, one red stack, and a four-row case card carrying the four exact field labels. No rule set has been mentioned.
Shot B2

Commit before the prices

Beat 2Tests the model
Persistent object
Strip and case card, both unchanged. Three option plates are added below the strip and stay for the rest of the act.
Learner prediction
The three plates carry the labels Rule 1 only, Rules 1 + 2, and All four Western Electric rules, and nothing else. The act stops with three empty total rows on screen and holds long enough for a viewer to choose out loud.
Transformation
Each plate previews its behaviour and never its price. The same recorded run replays across the strip three times, once per plate, and each replay lights the slot where that rule set first alarms. The single-rule replay alarms late. The all-four replay alarms early, and it also lights an alarm during a stretch of the same run where nothing had changed.
Surprise
The plate that alarms earliest is the same plate that alarms when nothing happened. Both consequences of the purchase are visible on the strip before either one has a price.
Counterexample
None. Withholding the totals is what makes this shot a test rather than a demonstration.
Formula reveal
None. A formula here would hand over the answer the viewer was just asked to commit to.
Readout source
challenge_case(...)["options"] supplies the three ids and their rule tuples, in the order rule-1, rules-1-2, all-four; first_violation() places the alarm slot on each replay; TRADE[key]["arl0"] sets how often the quiet-stretch alarm is drawn, so the false alarm shown is the rate that is later charged for.
Camera move
Locked wide on the three plates and the strip together. Cutting between plates would let the viewer forget that all three are reading one run.
Narration line
"Pick one now. The prices are off screen on purpose."
Exit state
Three plates, three lit alarm slots, three empty total rows. Nothing is scored and no option is marked.
Shot B3

Three prices out of one case

Beat 3Applies the model
Persistent object
Strip, case card, and the three plates. Each plate's empty total row becomes a bar and keeps it.
Learner prediction
None. The commitment was taken in B2, and this shot pays it off.
Transformation
Every total is built rather than printed. The amber alarm blocks and the red waiting blocks slide out of the strip and stack into one bar per plate, so each bar has two visibly different segments and each segment can be traced back to the slot it came from. All three bars are built from the same source blocks, one after another, which is what makes it obvious that one case is paying for all three answers.
Surprise
The tallest amber segment and the tallest red segment belong to different plates. The winning bar is not best at either half; it is the one that is worst at neither.
Counterexample
None in this shot. B4 is the counterexample and needs these three bars left standing.
Formula reveal
Only under the finished bars does the expected-cost expression write itself: expected false alarms times false-alarm cost, plus delay minutes times missed-shift cost. Each term is drawn in the colour of the segment it built, so the expression reads as a caption on two stacks that already exist.
Readout source
expected_cost() per option, and the printed totals from challenge_case(...)["options"][i]["expected_cost"]; false_alarm_run_length() sizes the amber segment and detection_delay() sizes the red one; challenge_case(...)["answer"] places the marker. No total is written into the scene as a literal.
Camera move
Locked while all three bars build, then one push to the shortest bar once the marker lands. The push happens after the comparison, never during it.
Narration line
"Same four facts, three prices. One is smaller, and that is arithmetic rather than taste."
Exit state
Three two-segment bars, the shortest marked, the expression under them, and the case card still lit beside the strip.
Shot B4

Move one line of the case

Beat 4Counterexample
Persistent object
Everything from B3, unchanged and uncut, including all three bars and all four case rows.
Learner prediction
The act dims three case rows, holds one of them bright, and asks which field it is about to change, before any bar moves. This rehearses the second half of the page challenge, which asks the learner to name the deciding field using these same four labels.
Transformation
Exactly one row changes: expected shift size moves from the case value to the large-shift value, and the curve above the strip slides out to match. The bars rebuild from the same blocks. The amber segments do not animate at all, because the false-alarm rate of a rule set does not depend on the shift. Only the red segments collapse, and the winner marker walks to a different bar.
Surprise
The amber halves are identical to the previous shot, frame for frame. The purchase price did not move at all. Only what the purchase bought moved, and that alone was enough to change the recommendation.
Counterexample
This shot is the counterexample, and it is the reason the act exists. Three fields are held visibly still, one field moves, and a different rule set becomes correct. The answer is therefore a property of this line, not a property of the Western Electric rules.
Formula reveal
None new. The expression from B3 stays exactly where it was, and only the inputs of its second term are re-lit, so the viewer can see which half of the arithmetic the changed field reached.
Readout source
The changed row moves from SHIFT to BIG_SHIFT, both of which are the two entries of SHIFT_GRID; expected_cost() is re-evaluated per option; the marker follows the recomputed challenge_case(...)["answer"]; deciding_field drives which row stays bright. The storyboard's expectation is that the winner moves from all-four to rule-1, and Task 3's counterfactual test must confirm that before this shot is rendered. If the computation disagrees, this shot changes, not the number.
Camera move
Locked, with the case card and all three bars in one frame. If the unchanged rows or the unchanged amber segments leave frame for even a moment, the argument of the shot is gone.
Narration line
"I changed one line of the case. The rules did not change. The answer did."
Exit state
Two priced worlds standing side by side from the same rules, with the marker on a different plate and one case row still bright.
Shot B5

What the next shift does

Beat 5Applies the model
Persistent object
The strip alone. Bars, plates, and case card fall away, and the strip returns to full width as a working chart.
Learner prediction
None. The act ends on an instruction. The unseen-data question belongs to the page challenge, which follows this act.
Transformation
The rule set that won the original case is applied to the strip and runs. Its alarm slot lights, and then three objects land on the strip in sequence: the affected run is held at the next step, the preceding subgroups are pulled back onto the chart, and the alarm is logged against the shift size it was bought to catch. Each action lands on the slot it applies to, so the sequence is a place on the line rather than a list.
Surprise
The final object on screen is not a number. The act ends with a line stopped, material held, and a reason written down.
Counterexample
None. B4 already carried it, and repeating it here would end the act on arithmetic.
Formula reveal
None, deliberately. Beat 5 forbids a formula recap, and the expression was already retired at the end of B4.
Readout source
challenge_case(CASE_SEEDS[0])["answer"] decides which rule set is applied; RULE_TEXT supplies the pattern the operator is now reading; first_violation() places the alarm slot on the running strip.
Camera move
One slow pull back to the whole strip, holding the alarm marker in frame to the last frame of the act.
Narration line
"You are not choosing a rule set. You are choosing what the next shift does when the chart alarms."
Exit state
One strip, one alarm, one held run, one logged reason, and no expression on screen.
Muted review gate

What a reviewer must be able to say with the sound off

If any line below needs the narration to be true, that shot fails and returns to this storyboard rather than to the render queue.

  • B1 This line has four facts, and the cost of waiting grows while the cost of a false alarm does not.
  • B2 More rules means an earlier alarm and also an alarm when nothing happened.
  • B3 All three prices came out of the same case, and the cheapest is the balanced one.
  • B4 One fact changed, the alarm cost stayed put, and a different option won.
  • B5 The chart alarmed, the line stopped, and somebody wrote down why.
Rejection list

Shots considered and cut

A storyboard with nothing rejected has not made a decision. Each cut below was a plausible shot, and each has a reason it does not exist.

  1. An opening slate naming the three rule sets before the case

    Cut. It would make the act about the rules, which is the belief B4 exists to break. The plates now arrive in B2, after the four case facts are standing on the strip as objects.

  2. A three-row, four-column cost table instead of the stacked bars

    Cut. A table lets a viewer compare totals without seeing which segment caused the difference, so the winner would have to be asserted. The B3 bars keep every number attached to the blocks that came off the strip.

  3. A sensitivity sweep across all four case fields

    Cut. Four moving premises prove nothing about which one decided the answer, and the challenge asks the learner to name exactly one field. B4 moves one field and holds the other three visibly still.

  4. A second masked case with a different winner

    Cut. It teaches the same beat a second time at full length, and B4 already proves the winner is case-dependent using one field. Variety belongs to the challenge's CASE_SEEDS bank, where it costs no runtime.

  5. A closing recap of the expected-cost expression

    Cut. Beat 5 requires a plant action, and a recap would return the act to arithmetic it had already finished. The expression retires at the end of B4 and never comes back.

  6. A named industry, site, or customer to make the case feel real

    Cut. The outcome contract forbids identifiers, and a named context invites the viewer to answer from memory of that context instead of from the four fields on the case card.

Lock condition

This storyboard is the input to Task 5, and its case fields are the input to Task 3. The four field labels and the three option labels on this page are the same strings the challenge and its feedback use. Changing one of them changes all three layers or none.
