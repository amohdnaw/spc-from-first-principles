SPC Level 7 · Act A · shot storyboard · locked before Manim

Every added signal rule buys earlier detection by spending a fixed stream of false alarms.

That sentence is the act's only visual claim. Six shots build it, test it, and hand one open question to Act B. A shot survives here only if it creates, transforms, tests, or applies the visual model. Runtime is not a gate. Every readout names the spclab symbol it reads; no number is typed into this document, because a number typed twice is a number that drifts.

Shot table

Six shots on one persistent object

The board created in A1 is the only stage. Nothing in this act is redrawn from scratch; every later shot transforms what is already there.

Shot A1

The line that will not move

Beat 1Creates the model
Persistent object
The board: one axis of the plotted statistic, the in-control curve standing on it, and one decision boundary drawn as a pair of verticals. The boundary is built by stepping out three equal sigma widths so it reads as a distance before it reads as a rule. This board stays on screen for the whole act.
Learner prediction
Motion stops with the boundary in place and one curve under it. The act asks the viewer to place the shifted mean where the chart would start noticing it, then holds a still frame long enough to commit. No answer is shown in this shot.
Transformation
A second copy of the same curve, identical in shape, slides right by one sigma of the plotted statistic and stops. Copy and slide, not a new draw, so the eye reads a moved process rather than a different process.
Surprise
Almost all of the shifted curve is still inside the boundary. The picture of a process that has genuinely moved looks like a picture of a process that has not.
Counterexample
None. The first shot only builds the object the rest of the act argues with.
Formula reveal
None. No symbol is allowed on screen yet; the boundary is labelled by its distance in sigma widths, which the viewer just watched being stepped out.
Readout source
LIMIT sets the boundary distance. SHIFT sets the slide distance. Both are read from spclab.evidence so the geometry and the later run lengths cannot disagree.
Camera move
Locked wide for the whole shot. The only motion in frame is the sliding curve, so the slide owns the viewer's attention.
Narration line
"Level 6 priced one way to be wrong. Here is the other one, in plain sight."
Exit state
Two curves and one boundary. No shading, no symbols, no readouts, and the viewer's prediction unanswered.
Shot A2

Two areas, then two letters

Beat 2Creates the model
Persistent object
The same board. Both curves and the boundary keep their positions; the shot adds fill, not geometry.
Learner prediction
None. The A1 prediction is still open, and asking a second question before answering the first would waste the commitment.
Transformation
Two fills sweep outward from the boundary itself. The in-control tail beyond the boundary fills amber. The body of the shifted curve inside the boundary fills alarm red. Because both sweeps start at the boundary, the boundary is visibly the thing that cuts one area from the other.
Surprise
The red area is most of a curve; the amber area is a sliver the viewer has to look for. The error that Level 6 priced is the small one, and the error nobody counts is the large one.
Counterexample
None. The contrast that carries this shot is between the two areas already in frame.
Formula reveal
Only after both areas hold still do the plain-language labels "crying wolf" and "staying silent" settle onto them, and only then does each label morph into a single letter. The symbols arrive last, on top of quantities the viewer has already watched being filled.
Readout source
ALPHA_1 for the amber area's value; BETA_AT[SHIFT] for the red area's value. ALPHA_1 comes from alpha_one_point(), which reads formulas.inside, so the sliver on screen and the rate quoted in section 7.1 are one computation.
Camera move
A slow push toward the boundary while the fills sweep, then a return to the wide framing before the labels morph. The push happens during the fill and never during the symbol reveal, so the symbols do not get a camera flourish they have not earned.
Narration line
"One of these is the error the limit was chosen against. The other is the one you are paying."
Exit state
Board, two curves, boundary, two filled areas, each carrying one symbol. The trade is on screen before any run length exists.
Shot A3

Sweeping the shift, watching the area

Beat 3Transforms the model
Persistent object
The board with both filled areas. A tracker handle is added on the shift axis at zero and becomes part of the stage for the rest of the act.
Learner prediction
With the handle at zero, the act asks the viewer to point at the shift size where the chart would catch half of what it is shown. The handle then starts moving from zero, so the viewer watches their own guess arrive or fail.
Transformation
The handle walks continuously from zero to three sigma. The shifted curve follows it. The red missed-shift area shrinks frame by frame while the portion of the shifted curve beyond the boundary grows, and that growing portion is mirrored into a rising column beside the board so power is a length as well as an area. The amber area does not change at any frame of the sweep.
Surprise
Power is flat for the whole first sigma and only wakes up near two. The half-way moment lands exactly when the shifted mean touches the boundary, which is the frame where the number explains itself without being said.
Counterexample
The sweep resets and repeats with the boundary pulled in to two sigma. Power rises much earlier, and the amber area fattens visibly while it does. This kills the obvious escape route before the rules are offered: sensitivity bought by moving the line is paid for in the same currency.
Formula reveal
Only after the handle returns to one sigma does the power expression write itself under the board, and each of its two terms lights the tail it names while it is written. The expression labels areas that have been on screen for the whole sweep.
Readout source
power_one_point() drives the continuous frames; POWER_AT supplies the tick values at the stops in SHIFTS; ARL1_FROM_POWER supplies the run-length echo at SHIFT. The two-sigma counterexample calls power_one_point(s, limit=2.0) and alpha_one_point(2.0), so the aside is computed by the same functions rather than sketched.
Camera move
Locked for the sweep, with one push at the half-way frame. The camera must not track the moving curve: if the boundary drifts in frame, the act loses the one thing that is holding still.
Narration line
"One point, one chance. That is the whole limitation."
Exit state
Handle parked at one sigma, the power column short, the power expression on screen under the board, and the boundary back where it started.
Shot A4

One point, two answers

Beat 4Tests the model
Persistent object
The board and the boundary. The two curves drop to outlines so that a single plotted point can hold the frame without the stage being rebuilt.
Learner prediction
A point drops at two and a half sigma and everything stops. The act asks for one word: in, or out. This is the prediction most viewers get right and still learn from.
Transformation
The point is answered twice, in two objects that cannot be mistaken for one label. The chart verdict prints in the boundary's own colour and reads as a pass. Then the point's own tail area peels off the board as a separate moving object and shrinks into an evidence chip. Two objects sit side by side, disagreeing, and neither is dismissed.
Surprise
The verdict says nothing happened. The peeled area says roughly one in eighty. The area that carried the evidence was drawn by the chart and then discarded by the chart's own rule.
Counterexample
The same routine runs again with the point at exactly three sigma, where the two objects agree and the evidence chip lands on the value already sitting on the amber area from A2. Agreement at that one point proves the disagreement everywhere else is a property of the rule and not of the drawing.
Formula reveal
The two-sided p-value expression writes itself along the peeled tail, after the chip exists. It names the area the viewer just watched leave the board.
Readout source
P_AT_2_5 on the chip; P_AT_3 and ALPHA_1 on the counterexample, which is where the two must be shown to be the same number; p_value() for any dragged intermediate position.
Camera move
Hold, tight on the point. The only motion in frame is the tail peeling off the board, so the peel carries the whole claim.
Narration line
"The verdict is not the evidence. The chart keeps one and throws the other away."
Exit state
A verdict object and an evidence chip on screen together, unresolved. That unresolved pair is the reason the next shot is allowed to spend money on rules.
Shot A5

The cost board, three stops

Beat 5Applies the model
Persistent object
The board shrinks into the upper third and stays there. A cost board takes the lower two thirds and holds it to the last frame of the act. It carries two bars on one shared axis of subgroups: the run of subgroups between false alarms, which the plant wants long, and the run of subgroups needed to catch a one-sigma shift, which the plant wants short.
Learner prediction
Both bars are frozen at their single-rule lengths before anything is switched on, and the act asks which way each one will move. The trap is that most viewers move one and hold the other.
Transformation
Three stops, each one a continuous slide of both bars and never a cut. At each stop the newly added rule draws its own pattern once on the chart above, so the bar motion has a visible cause on the chart rather than a caption. The stops are rule 1, rules 1 and 2, then all four.
Surprise
Both bars shorten together at every stop. The bar the plant wants long and the bar the plant wants short move the same way, and there is no stop anywhere on the board where one improves alone.
Counterexample
None in this shot. A6 is the counterexample and needs this board left standing to work.
Formula reveal
None, deliberately. The cost ratio and the sensitivity ratio are drawn as two comparison lengths laid along the bars' own travel, because both quantities are already lengths on screen and printing them as factors would replace a picture with a caption.
Readout source
TRADE[(1,)], TRADE[(1, 2)], and TRADE[ALL_RULES], each read at keys arl0 and arl1; cumulative_sets() for the stop order; RULE_TEXT for each pattern caption; FALSE_ALARM_COST and SENSITIVITY_GAIN for the two comparison lengths; CHAMP_WOODALL_ARL0 as an external tick set beside the all-four false-alarm bar.
Camera move
Locked on the cost board, with the chart above dimmed except in the moment its rule pattern draws. The published 1987 tick lands during a hold, with no camera move, so corroboration reads as a fact and not as a reveal.
Narration line
"Every rule you switch on buys time and spends alarms. Both sides are simulated, not asserted."
Exit state
Two bars at their all-four lengths, with the three stops left behind as ghost ticks on both. The whole purchase history stays visible in one frame.
Shot A6

Same purchase, different shift

CounterexampleTests the model
Persistent object
The cost board from A5, untouched, with all three ghost stops still on both bars.
Learner prediction
None. The board answered the A5 prediction thirty seconds ago; asking again here would be padding rather than a test.
Transformation
Exactly one thing changes. The shifted curve on the chart above jumps from one sigma to three, and the catch bar redraws its three stops for that shift. The false-alarm bar is not animated at all, because nothing about it depends on the shift.
Surprise
The catch bar barely shortens across its three stops. Rule 1 already sees a three-sigma jump on the next point, so the extra rules are bought at exactly the previous price and then never used.
Counterexample
This shot is the counterexample to the act's own claim as a viewer is likely to have over-generalised it. The rules did not get worse. The shift the plant is aiming at changed, and that alone decided whether the purchase was worth making.
Formula reveal
None. Introducing an expected-cost expression here would start Act B inside Act A.
Readout source
ARL_BIG_ONE and ARL_BIG_ALL at BIG_SHIFT for the redrawn catch bar, against ARL1_ONE_RULE and ARL1_ALL left ghosted at SHIFT; ARL0_ONE_RULE and ARL0_ALL unchanged on the false-alarm bar to prove the cost held still.
Camera move
Locked, framing both bars. The unmoving false-alarm bar has to stay in shot for the entire change, because its stillness is the argument. A cut to the catch bar alone would destroy the shot.
Narration line
"Same purchase. The cost did not move. The benefit nearly vanished."
Exit state
Two bars, four ghosted stops, and one question left standing: which shift is this line actually afraid of? Act B opens on that question. There is no summary card.
Muted review gate

What a reviewer must be able to say with the sound off

If any line below needs the narration to be true, that shot fails and returns to this storyboard rather than to the render queue.

  • A1 The process moved and the chart did not react.
  • A2 There are two errors here, they are areas, and the uncounted one is much bigger.
  • A3 A bigger shift is easier to catch, the gain is not linear, and moving the line instead just fattens the other area.
  • A4 This point passed and still carried evidence, and those are two separate things.
  • A5 Switching rules on shortens both runs, and one of those is bad news.
  • A6 Against a large shift the same purchase costs the same and buys almost nothing.
Rejection list

Shots considered and cut

A storyboard with nothing rejected has not made a decision. Each cut below was a plausible shot, and each has a reason it does not exist.

  1. A rules slate naming all four Western Electric rules up front

    Cut. It teaches the rules as a list to memorise, which is the exact failure this level was written against. The rule text now appears only at the stop in A5 where that rule's pattern causes a bar to move, so a name always arrives attached to a price.

  2. A power table with one row per shift in SHIFTS

    Cut. It converts the continuous sweep back into the lookup the level is arguing against, and beat 3 forbids it. The same values survive as tick marks on the A3 tracker, where they are positions rather than rows.

  3. A title card introducing alpha and beta before the areas exist

    Cut. It puts a symbol before its quantity, which the outcome contract forbids outright. A2 now morphs plain-language labels into symbols only after both areas have been filled and held.

  4. A closing card restating the trade after A6

    Cut. The two bars and their four ghost stops already show it, so the card would teach nothing and would end the act on a recap. A6 now ends on the open question that Act B is built to answer.

  5. A histogram that grows into the normal curve at the top of the act

    Cut. Level 4 already owns that derivation, and redrawing a concept the viewer has been given is the redundancy this rebuild exists to remove. A1 starts with the curve already standing.

  6. A live simulation of the run-length series accumulating

    Cut. The four thousand series behind TRADE cannot be drawn honestly at sixty frames per second, and a sped-up illustration would be motion decorating a number that was computed elsewhere. The simulation reaches the act only as its result.
